Did the onboarding redesign improve completion without sacrificing user quality?

I evaluated a bundled redesign of form burden, process guidance and question explanations. The decision combines experiment validity, full-funnel conversion, seven-day quality signals, complaint guardrails and channel rollout conditions.

Decision: staged channel rollout

The primary metric improves and short-term quality moves in the same direction. Immediate full release is not justified because the treatment bundles several changes and process-level guardrails remain incomplete.

Data disclosure: all 46,218 users and 221,046 event records are simulated. The project contains no employer, internship, client, brokerage, production, customer or confidential data.

Experiment design

Read the outcome, quality and validity layers together

Randomisation

User-level 50/50 assignment: 23,256 control users and 22,962 treatment users.

Inference

Two-sample proportion tests, 95% confidence intervals and channel heterogeneity with Holm correction.

Decision metrics

Completion is the primary metric; retention and first deposit test quality; complaints act as a guardrail.

Observation window: 75 days. Seven-day outcomes are read only for mature cohorts so late-arriving users do not mechanically depress the results.

Funnel diagnosis

Where the improvement occurs

Largest gain

Start → basic-information submission improves by 7.14pp, consistent with lower early form friction.

Supporting gain

Risk-assessment entry and completion improve, suggesting clearer guidance and explanations.

Remaining bottleneck

Bank-card binding → completion declines by 0.94pp. OTP, bank verification, KYC review and external redirects still require diagnosis.

Next experiment: separate form simplification, progress guidance and FAQ placement into individual treatments; investigate final-step verification friction independently.
Rollout governance

What must be true before scaling

Continue when

  • The completion confidence-interval lower bound remains above zero.
  • Retention and first deposit do not turn negative.
  • Complaint and support-cost guardrails remain stable.
  • Assignment, exposure and outcome logs form a valid chain.

Pause or roll back when

  • Sample-ratio mismatch or cross-device contamination appears.
  • OTP, card-binding or KYC failures increase materially.
  • Complaints or manual remediation costs rise.
  • New completions fail to convert into retained or funded accounts.
Seven-day metrics are short-term proxies. Thirty-day retention, first-month deposits, trading activity and lifetime value remain unvalidated.
